Implementing a knowledge graph can be a game-changer for your business, but there are definitely some traps you'll want to avoid. One common mistake is treating a knowledge graph as just another place to store data without a clear plan for how it'll be used. You need to know who will consume this data and what questions it will answer.

Without that clarity, you might end up with a powerful tool that nobody actually uses effectively. Another big one is poor entity definition. If you have multiple, differing definitions of something like 'customer' or 'order' across your systems, or if you lack unique identifiers, you're setting yourself up for duplication and inconsistent reports.

This makes it really hard for AI or reporting tools to give you reliable insights. Under-specifying relationships and hierarchies is also a frequent failure point. If your product categories aren't clearly defined or your customer hierarchies are fuzzy, you'll struggle to aggregate data or drill down into specifics when you need to.

And critically, neglecting to validate your entity files against your source systems, or not assigning clear ownership for ongoing updates, will lead to data quality issues piling up over time. It's like letting dust collect on a complex machine – eventually, it'll stop working right. If these challenges sound familiar or intimidating, bringing in a specialist can be incredibly helpful to navigate the complexities and ensure your knowledge graph is built to last and truly benefit your business.

Want to get your business cited more often by AI? Here's a quick checklist to help you get started on improving your AI citation rate. First up, identify target prompts and questions.

We suggest querying ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google, and Claude with 15-20 buyer-style questions related to your business. See who they cite and where the gaps are. Next, map your question landscape.

Use tools like 'People Also Ask' sections, analyze your support tickets, CRM notes, and relevant forums. This helps you figure out all the primary and secondary questions your audience is asking. Then, it's all about restructuring your content for AI extraction.

This means putting answer-first paragraphs (the first 40–80 words should be the direct answer!) , using Q&A-style H2/H3 headings, keeping paragraphs short (2–3 sentences), using bullet lists, and creating standalone sections. Focus on clarity and conciseness, remember, AI is looking for easy-to-digest chunks of information.

Don't forget to implement schema and technical foundations. Add FAQPage and Article schema to relevant pages. Make sure you include 'datePublished' and 'dateModified' tags, especially for content that's regularly updated.

Ensure your HTML is clean, your site has fast indexing via sitemaps, and AI crawlers can access your content without issues. Blocking bots or relying solely on JavaScript for schema can really limit your potential here. Lastly, you'll want to strengthen your trust and authority signals.

Include author credentials, external source citations, relevant statistics, and strong internal linking. Keep your entity names consistent across all platforms. Monitoring and iterating on these changes is key to seeing consistent improvement.

Voice search is a game-changer, and it's not something you can afford to ignore anymore. When someone asks their phone or smart speaker for a local service, they're not getting a long list of blue links – they're typically getting one spoken answer. That's high stakes! To be that chosen answer, your website needs to be perfectly optimized for how AI systems handle spoken queries. This isn't just about showing up; it's about being the clear, concise, and trustworthy source that AI can confidently read aloud. This checklist helps you get your site ready for voice search's unique demands.

How to use this checklist

Go through each item on this list for your key service pages. Each checked box means you're one step closer to being the answer AI systems deliver when prospects ask out loud.

  • Ensure important information is crawlable text — why: AI systems need to 'read' your content to understand it. If crucial details are stuck in images or complex interactive elements, voice assistants can't access them.
  • Strengthen authorship and sourcing on content pages — why: AI trusts content from credible sources. Clearly indicating who wrote the content and where facts come from builds this trust, helping AI confidently cite your business.
  • Validate structured data against visible content — why: Speakable schema tells AI exactly what parts of your content are meant to be read aloud. If your schema is inaccurate or doesn't match the on-page text, AI can get confused.
  • Define priority audiences and their specific questions — why: Voice search queries are often very specific. Knowing exactly what your ideal clients ask helps you tailor content to be a direct answer, increasing your chances of being chosen.
  • Map each question to one clear, authoritative page — why: AI prefers a single, definitive source for an answer. Having multiple, similar pages for the same question dilutes authority and can confuse voice assistants.
  • Improve headings, opening answers, and page organization — why: Voice assistants need short, clear, self-contained answers. Well-structured pages with direct answers at the top make it easier for AI to extract and speak them aloud.
  • Monitor voice search impressions and referrals — why: You can't improve what you don't measure. Keeping an eye on how your site performs in voice search provides valuable insights for ongoing optimization.

Then, research which schema.org types Google actually supports for rich results and enhanced features. Google recommends JSON-LD, so aim for that where you can. This is crucial: your schema markup must match what's visible to users on your page. Don't try to hide keywords or mark up information that isn't actually there. If you're using `Article` schema, make sure the `headline` and `author` properties directly correspond to the article's title and listed author. The goal is clarity and accuracy, providing AI with an unambiguous understanding of your content.

After adding the markup, use Google's Rich Results Test to check for errors and warnings specific to rich result eligibility. For broader schema.org validation, the Schema Markup Validator is your friend. This step is non-negotiable! Schema markup isn't a set-it-and-forget-it task. Search engine guidelines and your website content can change. Regularly recheck your reports in Search Console. Getting your website noticed by AI systems like Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, Siri, and Alexa is a whole new ballgame compared to traditional SEO. It's not just about keywords anymore; it's about being machine-readable, fact-dense, and structured in a way AI can easily digest. This checklist focuses on practical steps to boost your AI visibility. Keep in mind, this isn't a one-time fix, but an ongoing process to make your business the go-to answer.

How to use this checklist

Go through each item and mark it off. If you're unsure about any step, that's a sign it might be time to get some professional help. The goal is to create a website that AI systems trust and can cite confidently.

  • Ensure your `robots.txt` doesn't block important content — This file tells AI crawlers what parts of your site they can't access; accidentally blocking key pages or resources will prevent them from being seen at all. Make sure it's not inadvertently blocking CSS, JavaScript, or important HTML files.
  • Verify all critical pages are accessible without login — AI bots can't log in! Any public-facing page you want AI to cite must be accessible to anonymous crawlers without requiring a username or password.
  • Check for server errors (e.g., 404s, 500s) on key URLs — If your pages are returning errors, crawlers can't process them. Regularly audit your site for broken links or server response issues, especially for your most valuable content.
  • Submit a clean, up-to-date XML sitemap to Search Console — This acts as a roadmap for AI crawlers, helping them discover all the important pages on your site. Ensure it only contains canonical, indexable URLs and is free of errors.
  • Implement JSON-LD schema markup for key content — This is essential for AI understanding. Use types like `FAQPage`, `Article`, `Service`, and `LocalBusiness` to describe your content and business in a structured, machine-readable format. Google recommends JSON-LD for its flexibility.
  • Validate all structured data with Google's Rich Results Test — Before deploying, and regularly after, use this tool to catch any errors in your schema markup. Incorrect or incomplete schema is often ignored by AI systems.
  • Ensure your schema markup accurately reflects visible content — AI systems are smart; they'll notice if your schema describes content that isn't actually on the page. Only mark up what users can see and read.
  • Monitor Search Console's Page Indexing and Rich Results reports — These reports give you direct feedback from Google on how your site is being crawled and indexed, and if your rich results are eligible. Use this data to identify and fix issues promptly.
  • Check that your internal linking is logical and comprehensive — Strong internal linking helps AI crawlers discover all your pages naturally, reinforcing the structure and hierarchy of your site's content. Think about how a user would navigate and make sure crawlers can follow that path too.
  • Review how your pages render on mobile devices — AI systems, especially Google, prioritize mobile-first indexing. Ensure your content, including all JavaScript and CSS, renders correctly and quickly on mobile, as this impacts crawlability and perceived quality.

By systematically working through this checklist, you'll significantly improve your website's fundamental discoverability for AI, moving closer to becoming a trusted source for answer engines. When things get complex, especially with dynamic content or large sites, that's when a specialist can provide invaluable support to ensure everything is perfectly tuned for AI consumption.
