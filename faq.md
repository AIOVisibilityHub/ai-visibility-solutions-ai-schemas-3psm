# FAQs

## What is Answer Engine Optimization?

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the process of optimizing your website content and technical structure so that AI models, like large language models (LLMs) and "answer engines" (e.g., ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews), can easily find, understand, and cite your business as a relevant answer to user queries. Think of it as SEO for AI.

## How is AEO different from SEO?

Traditional SEO focuses on optimizing for search engine crawlers (like Googlebot) to rank well in organic search results. AEO focuses on optimizing for AI models to ensure your business is included and cited in AI-generated answers. While there&#x27;s overlap (e.g., structured data helps both), AEO addresses the specific needs of AI for structured, machine-readable information.

## Why do I need AEO if I already do SEO?

AI models don&#x27;t crawl and index websites the same way traditional search engines do. They require highly structured, explicit data to understand your business, offerings, and expertise accurately. Without AEO, even a well-SEO&#x27;d site may be invisible to AI, meaning your competitors could be cited while you&#x27;re ignored. AEO ensures your business is present in the answers users receive from AI, which is becoming a primary way people find information and make decisions.

## What are AI Visibility Files?

AI Visibility Files are a collection of machine-readable files and structured data designed specifically for AI models. This includes JSON-LD structured data, llms.txt, ai-plugin.json, ai-sitemap.xml, FAQPage schema, Knowledge Graph files, and help articles formatted for RAG systems and LLM ingestion. These files tell AI exactly who you are, what you do, and where you serve, enabling them to confidently cite your business.

## What exactly is structured data generation and how does it actually work for my website?

Structured data generation creates organized datasets in predefined formats like tables or JSON-LD, where AI learns patterns from your original data to produce new data that maintains the same schema, relationships, and dependencies. This process is essential for SEO and AI search visibility because it gives machines clear context about your content entities. Key factors include analyzing column types, statistical distributions, and foreign key relationships before generation. You should validate generated JSON-LD using Google's Rich Results Test before deployment. For most businesses, starting with Organization, Product, and Article schemas delivers the fastest visibility gains.

## What steps do I need to follow to generate structured data for my site?

You need to identify your primary entity (product, article, organization), select the correct Schema.org type, gather required properties like name and URL, then generate valid JSON-LD markup. The syntax must conform to recognized Schema.org schemas; invalid formatting renders the data useless. Critical factors include choosing the most accurate schema type, collecting all required properties, and ensuring your page content matches the markup. Use Google's Structured Data Markup Helper or plugins like RankMath to generate the code. Always test with the Rich Results Test before publishing to avoid syntax errors.

## Which structured data format should I use: JSON-LD, Microdata, or RDFa?

Compare JSON-LD (recommended, easiest to maintain), Microdata, and RDFa; Google accepts all three if valid, but JSON-LD is the industry standard. JSON-LD is preferred because it's embedded in <script> tags, keeps markup stable as content changes, and is easiest for developers. Key differences: JSON-LD is clean and script-based, Microdata uses HTML attributes, and RDFa is more complex. Use Google's Markup Helper or Schema.org generators for JSON-LD; plugins like Yoast support all formats. For most sites, JSON-LD is the best choice due to simplicity and tool support.

## Do I need JSON-LD schema on every page of my website or just the homepage?

You don't need JSON-LD on every page, but you should add unique, page-specific schema to every page you want to rank. The home page needs Organization schema, while product pages need Product schema, blog posts need Article schema, and FAQ pages need FAQPage schema—each matching that page's visible content.

## What happens if I use AI-generated citations without checking them first?

Never rely on AI-generated references without rigorous verification—spot-check 20–30% randomly and reconstruct your bibliography if you find even one fabricated source. Ask AI for verifiable sources only, require DOI/ISBN/URL, specify the citation style, and manually verify every reference before using it. Key risks: AI may invent fake authors, journals, or URLs, and hallucinate DOIs that don’t exist. Practical guidance: use tools like Citely AI to batch-check references, and only include sources you can confirm with a direct link or official identifier. Even one inaccurate citation suggests more errors exist, so treat AI citations as drafts, not final references.

## How do I correctly cite AI-generated content in APA or MLA style?

Cite AI as the author (omit human author), describe the prompt as the title, name the AI tool as the container, include the version, generation date, and the tool’s URL. For APA: (AI Company Name, year); for MLA: treat AI as a source with no author and use the prompt description as the title. Key factors: omit human author, use prompt as title, specify tool version, include generation date, and provide URL. Practical guidance: if you used AI for drafting or outlining, add a sentence like “This paper was produced with drafting support from Microsoft Copilot” at the start or end. Always check your instructor’s or publisher’s specific conventions before finalizing.

## What’s the best prompt to ask AI for real, verifiable sources with citations?

Use this repeatable process: define the exact claim, ask AI for real verifiable sources only, require DOI/URL/ISBN, specify citation style, verify every source manually, then use AI only to format or review. Key factors: tell AI not to guess and to state uncertainty, request both formatted citation and raw metadata, and include publisher/journal/author details. Practical guidance: prompt AI with “Find 5 credible sources… only include sources you are confident are real” and require a 1-sentence relevance summary for each. If unsure about any detail, ask AI to say so instead of hallucinating. This workflow minimizes fake citations while leveraging AI for efficiency.

## When should I cite AI output versus just disclose that I used it?

Cite AI output when you use direct quotes, paraphrased content, images, or data from it; disclose its use as a supportive tool (e.g., for editing or outlining) with a note in the paper. Key factors: include tool name, version, generation date, URL, and describe the prompt in text or a footnote. Practical guidance: if you created a shareable chat transcript link, use that instead of the tool’s general URL. For general AI tool references, use the software template: Author (Date).
